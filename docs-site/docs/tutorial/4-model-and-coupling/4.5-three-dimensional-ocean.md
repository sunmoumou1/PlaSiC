# 4.5 Three-Dimensional Ocean

The mixed-layer ocean (slab ocean) introduced in [4.4 Slab Ocean](4.4-slab-ocean.md) has only a single water layer with fixed heat capacity; it has no ocean currents, no stratification, and no deep-water heat storage capacity. It can reproduce seasonal-scale sea surface temperature memory, but it cannot represent several of the most important functions of the real ocean: transporting excess heat from the equator toward high latitudes, storing heat in the deep ocean through vertical stratification, and slowly releasing it on centennial scales. To supplement these processes while keeping the atmosphere model lightweight, PlaSiC attaches a **three-dimensional deep ocean** beneath the slab (`src/sea/3docean.c`; cf. Manabe, 1969; Bryan, 1969; Hansen et al., 1983; Fraedrich et al., 2005).

The goal of this note is not to retell a textbook, but to explain clearly, item by item, the equations, discretization, and coupling actually used by PlaSiC, so that readers can match them up when reading `3docean.c`, `physics_processes.c`, and `physics_io.c`.

!!! tip "Five things to remember first"

    1. **The surface is still a slab**: the SST seen by the atmosphere is provided by the slab, while the three-dimensional ocean handles the deep ocean below 50 m;
    2. **Temperature and salinity are prognostic variables**, horizontal velocity is a **diagnostic variable** (geostrophic balance + Ekman), and vertical velocity is derived from the continuity equation;
    3. The main processes of a single time step are: equation of state → diagnostic circulation → upwind advection and diffusion → convective adjustment;
    4. The slab and the deep ocean are **two-way coupled** through the top layer: the top layer is reset to the slab SST each step, and after one integration step the deep-ocean heat flux $Q_{\rm deep}$ is computed back;
    5. The three-dimensional ocean uses the **same Gaussian grid as the atmosphere**, is parallelized by latitude, skips entire land columns, and falls back to one-sided or zero differences at coastlines.

## 0. From Slab to Three-Dimensional Ocean: What Was Added

Recall the heat budget of the slab from [4.4 Section 1](4.4-slab-ocean.md#1-heat-budget). Over open water it is only

$$
C_o\frac{dT_o}{dt} = Q_{i\to o} + Q_{\rm deep}.
$$

The symbols are those of 4.3 and 4.4. $T_o$ is the slab temperature and $C_o=\rho_oc_{p,o}H$ its heat capacity. $Q_{i\to o}$ is the flux the sea-ice module hands down to the slab ([Figure 4.3.1, step 5](4.3-sea-ice-module.md)); over open water above the freezing point it is the atmospheric flux $Q_a$ less the latent heat of snowfall. $Q_{\rm deep}$ is the flux through the base of the slab. Under ice the slab is held at the freezing point, and both fluxes go to the ice inside $Q_{o\to i}$ instead. Without the three-dimensional ocean $Q_{\rm deep}=0$: the slab exchanges no heat through its base. The three-dimensional ocean computes $Q_{\rm deep}$ from a prognostic interior ocean: it stores temperature and salinity in multiple layers, can form stratification, undergo convection, and redistribute heat vertically and horizontally.

When understanding the three-dimensional ocean, always distinguish two levels:

| Level | What it handles | Prognostic variables | Directly visible to the atmosphere |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slab mixed layer | Rapid response to atmospheric fluxes, provides SST | $T_o$ | Yes |
| Three-dimensional deep ocean | Stores and transports deep-ocean heat and salinity | $T(x,y,z)$, $S(x,y,z)$ | No, only indirectly through $Q_{\rm deep}$ |

Therefore, the three-dimensional ocean **does not replace the slab**; rather, it turns the slab from an "isolated heat capacity" into a "mixed layer covering a real three-dimensional ocean".

## 1. State Variables and Vertical Grid

### 1.1 Main State

Each ocean grid point stores one vertical column, containing `PLASIC_DEEP_NLEV` layers. Each rank stores only the columns of its own latitude band plus one ring of ghost rows.

| Variable | Dimensions | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `temperature` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Prognostic | Potential temperature (K) |
| `salinity` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Prognostic | Salinity (g/kg) |
| `u_velocity`, `v_velocity` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Diagnostic | Horizontal velocity (m/s) |
| `w_velocity` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Diagnostic | Vertical velocity (m/s) |
| `density` | $N_{\rm hor}\times N_z$ | Diagnostic | Computed from the equation of state |
| `layer_thickness` | $N_z$ | Constant | Thickness of each layer (m) |
| `layer_center_depth` | $N_z$ | Constant | Center depth of each layer (m) |
| `deep_ocean_flux` | $\text{NLON}\cdot\text{NLPP}$ (no ghost rows) | Output | Deep-ocean heat flux returned to the slab |

where $N_{\rm hor}=\text{NLON}\cdot(\text{NLPP}+2)$, and the two extra rows are the northern and southern ghosts.

### 1.2 How the Vertical Grid Is Generated

`deep_ocean_build_levels()` generates the grid by growing the layer thickness linearly. Using a 1-based layer index $k$,

$$
\Delta z_k=
\begin{cases}
50\ \mathrm{m}, & k=1,\\
50+40(k-2)\ \mathrm{m}, & k\ge 2,
\end{cases}
\qquad
z_k=\sum_{j<k}\Delta z_j+\frac{\Delta z_k}{2}.
$$

The first layer is exactly the slab's 50 m mixed layer, so that the "top layer" of the three-dimensional ocean is geometrically aligned with the mixed layer seen by the atmosphere. With the default 16 layers, the center depths are

$$
25,\ 75,\ 145,\ 255,\ 405,\ 595,\ 825,\ 1095,\ \dots,\ 4695\ \mathrm{m},
$$

and the deepest layer interface is about 5000 m, close to the global mean ocean depth.

!!! tip "The number of layers can be adjusted by the user"

    The number of layers is determined by the compile-time macro `PLASIC_DEEP_NLEV`, and can be specified at build time with
    `make -C src run DEEP_OCEAN_LEVELS=22`; the build directory automatically gets an `_O22` suffix,
    avoiding mixing object files of different layer counts. The thickness formula holds for any number of layers.

### 1.3 Initial Stratification for Cold Start

At cold start there is no observed three-dimensional field, so the model fills it with an analytic profile (`deep_ocean_seed_state()`):

$$
T(z)=T_{\rm deep}+(T_o-T_{\rm deep})e^{-z/500},
\qquad T_{\rm deep}=\min(T_{\rm abyss},\,T_o),\quad T_{\rm abyss}=275.15\ \mathrm{K},
$$

$$
S(z)=34.7+0.2\left(1-e^{-z/1000}\right).
$$

That is, the surface takes the SST at that time and approaches 2 °C cold deep water exponentially with depth; salinity is about 34.7 in the upper layer and about 34.9 in the deep layer. Columns whose surface is colder than 2 °C (the polar seas, where the slab sits at the freezing point 271.25 K) are seeded isothermal at the surface temperature. Velocities start from rest. A restart run restores the real three-dimensional field from file.

The isothermal polar columns matter for sea ice. Until November 2026 every column relaxed to 2 °C at depth, so polar columns had cold, dense water on top of warm, light water. Because the top layer is reset to the slab temperature every step (Section 3.2), convective adjustment (Section 2.6) then kept mixing deep heat upward, and the deep-to-slab flux sat at the +2000 W m⁻² end of its clamp (Section 2.7): a three-dimensional cold start lost all of its initial sea ice within a few days and stayed ice-free for more than a year. With the isothermal seed, a two-year T21 cold start keeps a seasonal Arctic ice cover of about 3.5–9.7 × 10⁶ km². The salinity profile alone keeps the isothermal columns stably stratified. Runs that started before the change, such as the 500-year spin-up of [6.2](../6-experiments/6.2-historical-experiment-1850-2014.md), used the old seed; after centuries of integration the deep ocean no longer remembers it.

## 2. Governing Equations

The three-dimensional ocean adopts an **incompressible, hydrostatic, Boussinesq-approximated** ocean framework (Bryan, 1969; Gill, 1982; Griffies, 2004). But PlaSiC makes one key simplification: **the momentum equations are not time-integrated**; the horizontal velocity is directly diagnosed from the density field. Thus there are only two prognostic equations: temperature and salinity. Throughout, $z$ is depth, positive downward as in Section 1.2, and the vertical velocity $w$ is also positive downward.

### 2.1 Equation of State (Linear EOS)

Density is approximated with a linear equation of state (Gill, 1982; `seawater_density()`):

$$
\rho=\rho_0\left[1-\alpha\,(T-T_{\rm ref})+\beta\,(S-S_{\rm ref})\right],
\qquad
T_{\rm ref}=4\ ^\circ\mathrm{C},\quad S_{\rm ref}=35.
$$

| Parameter | Default value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| $\rho_0$ | $1030\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ | Reference density |
| $\alpha$ | $2.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$ | Thermal expansion coefficient |
| $\beta$ | $7.5\times10^{-4}\ (\mathrm{g\,kg^{-1}})^{-1}$ | Haline contraction coefficient |

The linear EOS ignores the effect of pressure on density, so the "potential density" here is an approximate potential density. Its greatest advantages are simplicity and stability, and it is sufficient to drive convective adjustment and geostrophic flow.

### 2.2 Hydrostatic Balance and Pressure

The horizontal velocity requires a pressure gradient. With $z$ measured downward, hydrostatic balance reads $\partial p/\partial z=\rho g$. Integrating downward from $p=0$ at the sea surface gives

$$
p_k=g\sum_{j\le k}\rho_j\Delta z_j-\tfrac12 g\rho_k\Delta z_k .
$$

The second term corrects the pressure from the layer interface to the layer center. Land grid points take $\rho=\rho_0$ and $p=0$, but they never enter the differences of ocean grid points (see Section 4.3).

### 2.3 Geostrophic Balance and the Ekman Surface Layer

On the rotating Earth, the large-scale ocean approximately satisfies geostrophic balance (Ekman, 1905; Gill, 1982). Writing the horizontal pressure gradient as

$$
u=-\frac{1}{\rho_0 f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y},
\qquad
v=+\frac{1}{\rho_0 f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x},
$$

where the Coriolis parameter is

$$
f=2\Omega\sin\varphi,\qquad \Omega=\frac{2\pi}{T_{\rm sidereal}}.
$$

$f$ is zero at the equator, which would cause division-by-zero amplification. The model limits $|f|$ to be at least $2\Omega\sin 2^\circ$, i.e. it considers the "effective $f$" to stop decreasing near the equator.

The sea surface also requires a wind-stress-driven Ekman flow. The model adds it directly to the top layer:

$$
u^{(0)}\mathrel{+}=\frac{\tau_y}{\rho_0 f h_0},
\qquad
v^{(0)}\mathrel{-}=\frac{\tau_x}{\rho_0 f h_0},
\qquad h_0=50\ \mathrm{m}.
$$

Here $u^{(0)},v^{(0)}$ are the top-layer velocities and $\tau_x,\tau_y$ the zonal and meridional wind-stress components. The form follows from the steady momentum balance in the surface boundary layer, where the Coriolis force is balanced by the vertical divergence of the turbulent stress:

$$
-f\,v=\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial \tau_x}{\partial z},
\qquad
f\,u=\frac{1}{\rho_0}\frac{\partial \tau_y}{\partial z}.
$$

Integrating vertically from the base of the Ekman layer (where the stress vanishes) to the surface gives the depth-integrated Ekman transport per unit width,

$$
M_x=\int u\,\mathrm{d}z=\frac{\tau_y}{\rho_0 f},
\qquad
M_y=\int v\,\mathrm{d}z=-\frac{\tau_x}{\rho_0 f}.
$$

The model then converts this transport into a mean velocity spread over the fixed 50 m surface layer by dividing by $h_0$, i.e. $u^{(0)}=M_x/h_0$ and $v^{(0)}=M_y/h_0$, which yields exactly the expressions above. Because PlaSiC diagnoses rather than time-integrates momentum, this Ekman velocity is simply added to the geostrophic velocity already obtained for the top layer.

![The Ekman spiral in the surface ocean layer](<../assets/images/Ekman_layer.jpg>)

Figure 4.5.2. Schematic of Ekman transport. Ekman transport is the net motion of a fluid resulting from a balance between the Coriolis force and turbulent drag forces. In the picture above, the wind blowing northward in the Northern Hemisphere creates a surface stress, and the resulting Ekman spiral develops below it in the water column. The Ekman layer is the layer in a fluid where the flow results from a balance among the pressure gradient, the Coriolis force, and turbulent drag forces. Image: Nedtheprotist, [*Ekman layer.jpg*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ekman_layer.jpg), Wikimedia Commons, public domain; used in the Wikipedia article [Ekman transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekman_transport).

All horizontal velocities are finally capped at $\pm0.5\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ to ensure explicit advection stability.

!!! warning "Velocity is a diagnostic variable, not a prognostic variable"

    There is no $du/dt$, no $dv/dt$, and no Coriolis tendency or inertial oscillation in the code. Each time step re-diagnoses $u,v$ from the **current**
    density field. This makes the ocean circulation respond instantaneously (without inertia) to changes in stratification. It is the main difference from
    primitive-equation ocean models such as that of Bryan (1969), which integrate $du/dt$ and $dv/dt$. The LSG model (Maier-Reimer et al., 1993), which
    `3docean.c` takes as its physical guide, also drops the inertial terms, but unlike PlaSiC it solves for the barotropic flow.

### 2.4 Continuity Equation and Vertical Velocity

Under incompressibility, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf u=0$, so $\partial w/\partial z=-(\partial u/\partial x+\partial v/\partial y)$ with $w$ positive downward. The model integrates the horizontal divergence downward from $w_0=0$ at the sea surface:

$$
w_k=w_{k-1}-\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)_{k-1}\Delta z_{k-1}.
$$

The running value is limited to $\pm10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ at every level. The vertical velocity of the real ocean is also of order $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, so this upper bound is physically reasonable; its main role is to prevent diagnostic divergence errors from being amplified into spurious strong vertical transport after cumulative integration.

### 2.5 Conservation Equations for Temperature and Salinity

Temperature and salinity are predicted with **advection–diffusion** equations (Bryan, 1969; Pacanowski & Philander, 1981):

$$
\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
=-\left(u\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial T}{\partial y}+w\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\right)
+\kappa_h\nabla_h^2 T
+\frac{1}{\Delta z_k}\left[\frac{\kappa_v(T_{k-1}-T_k)}{\delta_{k-\frac12}}
+\frac{\kappa_v(T_{k+1}-T_k)}{\delta_{k+\frac12}}\right].
$$

The salinity equation has exactly the same form. The interface distances are

$$
\delta_{k\pm\frac12}=\tfrac12(\Delta z_k+\Delta z_{k\pm1}).
$$

| Parameter | Default value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| $\kappa_v$ | $2.0\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Vertical diffusion coefficient |
| $\kappa_h$ | $500\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$ | Horizontal diffusion coefficient |

Horizontal diffusion is used to suppress grid-point noise on the coarse grid; vertical diffusion slowly transfers surface heat and water-mass properties downward.

### 2.6 Convective Adjustment

Advection and diffusion may cause density to decrease with depth (hydrostatic instability). The model removes this instability using **convective adjustment** (Bryan, 1969; Marotzke, 1991): scanning from top to bottom, if

$$
\rho_k<\rho_{k-1}-10^{-4}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}
$$

and both layers have positive salinity, then the two adjacent layers are mixed, weighted by layer thickness:

$$
T_{k-1}=T_k=\frac{T_{k-1}\Delta z_{k-1}+T_k\Delta z_k}{\Delta z_{k-1}+\Delta z_k}.
$$

Salinity is handled by the same formula, and then the density is recomputed. The scan is repeated until a pass mixes nothing, but at most $N_z$ times, so a column can keep an inversion smaller than $10^{-4}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. It replaces the finite velocity of convective plumes in the real ocean with instantaneous mixing completed within one time step.

### 2.7 Freshwater, Freezing Point, and Clamping

The salinity of the top layer is affected by the freshwater flux $F_w$ (precipitation minus evaporation, in m/s). The freshwater introduced in one time step dilutes the salinity:

$$
S^{(0)}\leftarrow S^{(0)}\frac{h_0}{h_0+F_w\Delta t}.
$$

The temperature does not fall below the seawater freezing point $T_f=271.25\ \mathrm{K}$. To avoid numerical divergence, the code also applies conservative clamping to the following quantities:

| Variable | Range |
|---|---|
| $T$ | $[T_f,\ 320\ \mathrm{K}]$ |
| $S$ | $[0,\ 60]\ \mathrm{g/kg}$ |
| $\lvert u\rvert,\lvert v\rvert$ | $\le 0.5\ \mathrm{m/s}$ |
| $\lvert w\rvert$ | $\le 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ |
| $Q_{\rm deep}$ | $\pm 2000\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$ |

These clamps are numerical safety nets. The old cold-start seed of Section 1.3 is a reminder that they can act for long periods: it held $Q_{\rm deep}$ at its upper limit.

## 3. Two-Way Coupling with the Slab

This is the part of the whole design that most needs careful understanding, and it is the key to reading `physics_ocean3d_advance()` and `ocean_mksst_*()`.

### 3.1 The Slab Side: One Extra Term Q_deep

The heat budget of the slab is still that of [4.4 Section 1](4.4-slab-ocean.md#1-heat-budget) (Section 0 above), except that $Q_{\rm deep}$ now comes from the three-dimensional ocean:

$$
C_o\frac{dT_o}{dt}=Q_{i\to o}+Q_{\rm deep}.
$$

Under ice, $Q_{\rm deep}$ is passed to the ice as part of $Q_{o\to i}$ instead.

### 3.2 The Deep-Ocean Side: The Top Layer Is Reset to the Slab SST

At the beginning of each step of the three-dimensional ocean, the top layer is set directly to the slab temperature:

$$
T^{(0)}\leftarrow T_o .
$$

This lets the 50 m mixed layer be governed by the atmosphere–slab system, and the deep ocean can affect it only through diffusion, convection, and circulation.

### 3.3 Computing Q_deep Back

After the top layer is reset, the model integrates the three-dimensional ocean one step, obtaining the new top-layer temperature $T^{(0)}_{\rm new}$. The difference between it and the slab temperature is converted into the deep-ocean heat flux:

$$
Q_{\rm deep}=\frac{\rho_0 c_{p,0}\left(T^{(0)}_{\rm new}-T_o\right)h_0}{\Delta t}.
$$

Here $\rho_0c_{p,0}h_0$ is the heat capacity of the top layer, with $c_{p,0}=4180\ \mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}$. With the default $\rho_0$, $c_{p,0}$ and $h_0=H=50\ \mathrm{m}$ it equals the slab's $C_o$.

This heat then enters the slab heat budget at the same coupling instant.

## 4. Numerical Methods

### 4.1 Time Discretization and Sub-cycling

Temperature and salinity use explicit time stepping:

$$
T^{n+1}=T^n+\Delta t\cdot\mathcal{T}(T^n,S^n,u,v,w),
$$

where $\mathcal{T}$ is the tendency from Section 2.5. Explicit upwind advection is constrained by the CFL condition (Courant et al., 1928):

$$
\Delta t\left(\frac{|u|}{\Delta x}+\frac{|v|}{\Delta y}+\frac{|w|}{\Delta z}\right)\lesssim1.
$$

The model subdivides $\Delta t$ into several sub-steps using a conservative estimate based on the velocity upper bound:

$$
n_{\rm sub}=\operatorname{clamp}\!\left(1+\left\lfloor 4\Delta t\left(\frac{0.5}{10^5}+\frac{10^{-6}}{50}\right)\right\rfloor,\ 1,\ 16\right).
$$

With the default coupling the ocean step equals the atmospheric step (30 minutes or shorter with the default steps), so this gives $n_{\rm sub}=1$; sub-cycling begins only when the ocean step exceeds about 14 hours. This is an engineering stability estimate, not the true CFL number computed step by step: with the assumed 0.5 m s⁻¹ over 100 km, the horizontal Courant number at a 30-minute step is only about 0.01, so a single sub-step is ample.

### 4.2 Spatial Discretization: Finite Differences and Upwinding

- Horizontally, the same Gaussian grid as the atmosphere is shared; the longitude direction is periodic, and the latitude direction uses extended ghost rows;
- Advection uses **first-order upwinding** (Haltiner & Williams, 1980): taking a backward difference along the flow direction, for example when $u>0$,
  $\partial T/\partial x\approx(T_i-T_{i-1})/\Delta x$;
- Diffusion uses second-order central differences;
- Vertical diffusion is written in the divergence form of interface fluxes, with the interface distances taken as the distances between adjacent layer centers.

Although first-order upwinding is only first-order accurate, it is positive-definite and does not generate new extrema, making it the most robust choice for a coarse-resolution ocean.

### 4.3 Land Mask and Coastal Differences

Horizontal differences must avoid bringing land values into the ocean. The model handles three cases (`zonal_gradient()`, `meridional_gradient()`):

| Neighbors on both sides | Difference scheme |
|---|---|
| Both ocean | Central difference |
| One side land | One-sided difference using the ocean side |
| Both land | Gradient set to 0 |

Upwind advection sets the gradient in a direction to zero when the upwind neighbor is land, so no land value is carried into the ocean; the velocity itself is not set to zero. Horizontal diffusion replaces the value at a land neighbor by that of the ocean grid point itself, which is equivalent to a zero-gradient condition. In this way, even if land grid points store arbitrary values, they can never affect the adjacent ocean.

![Horizontal decomposition, halo exchange, and land mask](<../assets/images/ocean3d-horizontal-halo.svg>)

Figure 4.5.4. The Gaussian grid is split into latitude bands, one per rank. Adjacent ranks exchange ghost rows, and coastal differences fall back to one-sided or zero gradients according to the land mask.

### 4.4 Polar Treatment

The Gaussian grid does not include the exact poles. The northernmost and southernmost ghost rows have no neighbors, so the model uses linear extrapolation to give their sine latitudes:

$$
\sin\varphi_{0}=2\sin\varphi_1-\sin\varphi_2,
\qquad
\sin\varphi_{N+1}=2\sin\varphi_N-\sin\varphi_{N-1}.
$$

In this way the meridional difference $\sin\varphi_{k-1}-\sin\varphi_{k+1}$ remains finite in the polar regions and does not divide by zero.

### 4.5 Ghost Rows at Process Boundaries

Every meridional stencil at an edge row reads one ghost row, so the ghost rows must hold the neighbouring rank's values whenever the step has changed them. Before each step, `physics_ocean3d_advance()` exchanges every level of $T$, $S$, $u$, $v$ and $w$. Within the step, `deep_ocean_step()` refreshes them through the `exchange_halo` hook of its configuration (`refresh_ghost_rows()` in `src/sea/3docean.c`, backed by `ocean3d_exchange_halo()` in the physics layer) at three points:

| When | Fields | Why |
|---|---|---|
| After the top-layer reset (Section 3.2) | top level of $T$, $S$ | density, pressure and advection at an edge row read the neighbour's new surface values |
| After the velocity diagnosis | every level of $u$, $v$ | the column divergence that gives $w$ needs the neighbour's new $v$ |
| After each tracer sub-step | every level of $T$, $S$ | the next sub-step's advection and diffusion read the neighbour's new tracers |

Only the global polar ghost rows, which have no neighbour, are filled by mirroring the adjacent interior row (zero gradient). With a single rank, or if the exchange fails, every ghost row is mirrored.

!!! warning "Fixed in November 2026"

    Until November 2026 only the full exchange before the step reached the neighbouring rank. Inside the step, the ghost rows were mirrored on every rank, and only for the top level of $T$ and $S$: after the top-layer reset and between sub-steps. The new $u$ and $v$ were not refreshed at all. At a process boundary the mirroring replaced the neighbour's surface row by the rank's own edge row, so the meridional gradient there was zero and the top layer had no meridional advection or diffusion across the boundary. A four-rank T85 integration developed SST steps of about 2 K at 45°N and 45°S, two of its three internal boundaries (the third is the equator). With the exchange, the result no longer depends on the number of ranks: after 64 steps a four-rank T21 run is bit-for-bit identical to the serial run, while the old code already differed by about 10⁻² K at the boundary rows. Two-rank runs had a single boundary at the equator; the 500-year spin-up and the control and historical runs of [6.2](../6-experiments/6.2-historical-experiment-1850-2014.md) were made before the fix.


## 5. Restart and Monthly Mean Output

### 5.1 Restart Records

The complete state of the three-dimensional ocean is written to restart, with records named

| Record | Content |
|---|---|
| `deep_nlev` | Number of layers, used for version checking |
| `deept` / `deeps` | Temperature / salinity |
| `deepu` / `deepv` / `deepw` | The three velocity components |

When reading an old restart, if these records are missing (for example, the single-layer restart of [4.4 Slab Ocean](4.4-slab-ocean.md)), the model **does not error out**; instead it rebuilds the three-dimensional ocean from the slab SST using the analytic profile of Section 1.3, without printing any message. If `deep_nlev` is inconsistent with the currently compiled number of layers, it likewise falls back to cold-start rebuilding. The in-memory fields are halo-extended, and are packed into contiguous local slices before writing to disk (`physics_ocean3d_pack_local()`).

### 5.2 Monthly Mean NetCDF

The monthly output adds two three-dimensional variables with dimensions `(time, depth, lat, lon)`:

| Variable | Standard name | Units | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `thetao` | `sea_water_potential_temperature` | K | Three-dimensional potential temperature |
| `so` | `sea_water_salinity` | 1e-3 | Three-dimensional salinity |

The vertical coordinate `depth` is the real ocean depth (m, `positive="down"`), taken from the layer center depths generated in Section 1.2, rather than the atmospheric sigma coordinate. Land grid points are filled with `_FillValue` layer by layer according to the surface land–sea mask.

The monthly files are written only when `plasic.x` gets `--monthly-output DIR`. `make historical` passes this option, but `make run` does not, and `make` would take the option as its own. Run the executable directly instead ([1.1 Section 1.4](../1-user-guide/1.1-Running-PlaSiC-first.md#14-running-the-executable-directly)) and create the directory first, since `plasic.x` does not create it. For example, from `src/`, a 31-day T31 cold start (48 steps per day):

```bash
mkdir -p ../run/demo/monthly
../build/T31_L13_MPI_P2_O16/plasic.x \
  --data-dir data/surface \
  --co2-ppm 284.3 \
  --steps 1488 \
  --monthly-output ../run/demo/monthly \
  --output ../run/demo/status
```

Only complete months are written; a month cut short by the start or end of the run is discarded.

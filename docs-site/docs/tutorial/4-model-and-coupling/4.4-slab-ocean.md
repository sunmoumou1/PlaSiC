# 4.4 Slab Ocean

PlaSiC's slab ocean is a single mixed layer with a prescribed depth and finite heat capacity. It provides the atmosphere-facing sea surface temperature (SST). It has no prognostic current, salinity, stratification, or deep-ocean storage, and vertical and horizontal diffusion are disabled: each ocean point only exchanges heat with the atmosphere, the sea ice, and the deep ocean. The standard configuration also uses a three-dimensional ocean to supply a prognostic deep-ocean heat flux; it is documented in [4.5 Three-Dimensional Ocean](4.5-three-dimensional-ocean.md). The inputs, the four settings that change the path, and the order of one ocean step in `physics_processes.c` are shown in the flow chart below.

## 1. Heat budget

The only prognostic variable is the mixed-layer temperature $T_o$. With the default mixed-layer depth $H=50\ \mathrm{m}$, the heat capacity per unit area is

$$
 C_o=\rho_o c_{p,o}H=1030\times4180\times50\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-2}\,K^{-1}}\approx2.15\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{J\,m^{-2}\,K^{-1}}.
$$

The slab is stepped once per sea-ice step, so $\Delta t$ is the step of [4.3 Thermodynamic Sea Ice](4.3-sea-ice-module.md). Two fluxes reach it: the flux handed down by the ice module and the deep-ocean flux $Q_{\rm deep}$, both positive into the ocean. $Q_{\rm deep}$ is computed by the three-dimensional ocean and is zero in a slab-only build.

Over open water ($h_i=0$ after the ice update), the flux handed down is the atmospheric net heat flux $Q_a$, less the latent heat of any snow that falls on open water ([4.3 Section 4](4.3-sea-ice-module.md#4-snow-melt-and-snow-to-ice-conversion)). Where the ice has just melted completely, it is $Q_f$ plus the melting energy left over (Section 2). The two fluxes change the temperature directly:

$$
 T_o^{*}=T_o^n+\frac{\Delta t}{C_o}\left(Q_a+Q_{\rm deep}\right).
$$

Under ice ($h_i>0$), the temperature does not respond to these fluxes. The ice module hands down only $Q_f=K_z(T_f-T_o)$, its share of the conductive flux ([4.3 Section 5](4.3-sea-ice-module.md#5-ice-thickness-energy-ledger)). This flux and $Q_{\rm deep}$ are passed on to the ice, so $T_o^*=T_o^n$.

Wherever there is ice, and where open water started the step below the freezing point $T_f=271.25\ \mathrm{K}$, the slab then converts the departure from $T_f$ into latent heat of ice and sets $T_o^{n+1}=T_f$. The flux returned to the ice is

$$
 Q_{o\to i}=
 \begin{cases}
 Q_f+Q_{\rm deep}+\dfrac{C_o}{\Delta t}\left(T_o^{*}-T_f\right), & \text{under ice},\\[8pt]
 \dfrac{C_o}{\Delta t}\left(T_o^{*}-T_f\right), & \text{open water with } T_o^n<T_f,\\[8pt]
 0, & \text{open water with } T_o^n\ge T_f,
 \end{cases}
$$

where a positive $Q_{o\to i}$ melts ice and a negative one forms ice. Under ice the correction always applies, so the slab stays at $T_f$. Over open water the test uses the temperature at the start of the step, as in PlaSim. A point that cools below $T_f$ during one step therefore keeps that SST until the next step converts the deficit into ice. On open water with $T_o^n\ge T_f$, the slab simply takes $T_o^{n+1}=T_o^*$.

Without a prognostic ocean the SST is set to climatology. With `ocean.flux_correction = 1` (off by default), the correction under ice is replaced by a restoring flux toward the climatological SST. Figure 4.4.1 shows these paths.

## 2. Coupling with sea ice

The slab runs inside the sea-ice step, after the ice has been updated. At a sea-ice coupling time, the order is:

1. the atmosphere provides the heat flux $Q_a$, averaged over the coupling window, together with snowfall, freshwater and wind stress;
2. the ice module computes the surface temperature, snow melt, and conductive flux, using the $T_o$ of the previous step;
3. the ice module updates the thickness with the $Q_{o\to i}$ that the slab returned at the previous step;
4. the ice module hands its ocean flux to the slab; the deep ocean and the slab then step forward, apply the heat budget of Section 1, enforce $T_f$, and return a new $Q_{o\to i}$ that the ice consumes at the next step;
5. the sea interface sends $T_i$, the new $T_o$, cover, thickness, and snow back to the atmosphere.

The freezing-point correction is therefore an energy transfer, not an arbitrary temperature limiter. Because the slab runs after the ice, the ice receives $Q_{o\to i}$ one step late; [4.3 Section 5](4.3-sea-ice-module.md#5-ice-thickness-energy-ledger) shows where this lag enters the ice ledger. Energy left over after the ice has melted completely moves the other way within the same step. The ice kernel adds it to the flux handed down in step 4, and the slab can warm in the same step.

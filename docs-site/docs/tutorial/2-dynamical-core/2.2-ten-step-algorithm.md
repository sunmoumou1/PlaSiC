# 2.2 Ten-Step Algorithm

## 1. What dynamical core solves and what it does not

An atmospheric model usually separates fluid motion on resolvable scales from subgrid processes:

- The **dynamical core** solves the transport of mass, momentum, thermal energy, and water vapor;
- **Physical parameterizations** describe processes that are not explicitly resolved: radiation, boundary-layer turbulence, convection, condensation, precipitation, surface exchange, and so on;
- The **coupler** exchanges fluxes among the atmosphere, ocean, sea ice, land surface, and vegetation.

`src/dynamical_core/calgp.c` holds the adiabatic nonlinear diagnostics within a grid-point column, while `src/dynamical_core/plasic_kernels.c` contains the remaining dynamical-core operators, numerical dissipation, Rayleigh friction, Newtonian cooling, and auxiliary operators that return dissipated kinetic energy as heat. They do not compute the parameterizations of clouds, radiation, or precipitation themselves; instead, they convert the tendencies produced by those modules into the dynamical core's internal variables and add them to the time advance.

## 2. Discrete notation and time levels

| Notation | Meaning in space or time |
|---|---|
| \(\widehat X_{n,k}^{m,j}\) | spherical harmonic coefficient at the current time level \(j\); \((n,m)\) is the horizontal mode and \(k\) is the full \(\sigma\) level; the two-dimensional field \(\pi\) has no \(k\) index |
| \(X_{{\rm gp},k}^{j}\) | the same current state synthesized onto the Gaussian grid |
| \(r_{X,{\rm gp}}\), \(\widehat R_X\) | grid-point tendency and spectral tendency, respectively |
| \(\widetilde{\widehat X}^{\,j-1}\) | old level after Robert–Asselin filtering, used for the current leapfrog update |
| \(\widehat X_*^{j+1}\) | candidate new-level spectral state after adding adiabatic dynamics only |
| \(\widehat X_{\rm phys}^{j+1}\) | spectral state after the physics tendencies are added to the candidate state |
| \(\widehat X^{j+1}\) | complete new-level spectral state after friction, dissipation are added |

In addition, \(\lambda_n=n(n+1)\), and \(k+\tfrac12\) denotes the interface between adjacent full \(\sigma\) levels. To avoid reversing the sign of the surface pressure equation, \(\widehat{\mathcal A}\) always denotes the spectral coefficient of \(\mathcal A=\int_0^1A\,d\sigma=-\partial\pi/\partial\tau\); it is never written as \(\widehat R_\pi\). Unless stated otherwise, the diagnostics and tendencies in Steps 1–4 are computed from the current level \(\widehat X^j\).

## 3. The ten steps of one time advance

**Step 1: Recover the grid-point state from the spectral state**

A vector spherical harmonic inversion of \((\widehat\zeta^{\,j},\widehat D^{\,j})\) gives \((U_{\rm gp}^{j},V_{\rm gp}^{j})\), and separate scalar syntheses give \(\zeta_{\rm gp}^{j}\) and \(D_{\rm gp}^{j}\). Synthesizing \(\widehat{T'}^{\,j}\), \(\widehat\pi^{\,j}\), and \(\widehat\chi^{\,j}\) likewise gives \(T'_\mathrm{gp}{}^{j},\pi_\mathrm{gp}^{j},\chi_\mathrm{gp}^{j}\). The surface pressure gradient $(\nabla_h\pi)_{\rm gp}^{j}$ is also computed from the current spectral coefficients \(\widehat\pi^{\,j}\) alone.

**Step 2: Compute the grid-point nonlinear dynamics**

For each Gaussian grid point, the program processes all \(\sigma\) levels at that point together. The table below lists all quantities belong to the current time level \(j\).

| Location | Diagnostic or output | Mathematical form or role |
|---|---|---|
| Full level \(k\) | \(P_{{\rm gp},k}^j\) | \(\mathbf v_{h,{\rm gp},k}^j\!\cdot(\nabla_h\pi)_{\rm gp}^j\) |
| Full level \(k\) | \(A_{{\rm gp},k}^j\) | \(D_{{\rm gp},k}^j+P_{{\rm gp},k}^j\) |
| Whole column | \(\mathcal A_{\rm gp}^j\) | \(\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Delta\sigma_k A_{{\rm gp},k}^j\), the full column integral used by \(\dot\sigma\) |
| Whole column | \(\mathcal A_{\rm gp}^{\rm exp,j}\) | \(\sum_{k=1}^{L}\Delta\sigma_k P_{{\rm gp},k}^j\), the explicit part analyzed for the surface pressure equation; the \(D\)-part is added in Step 4 |
| Full level \(k\) | \(q_{{\rm gp},k}^j\), \(T_{{\rm gp},k}^j\) | \(\max(\chi_{{\rm gp},k}^j e^{-\pi_{\rm gp}^j},0)\), \(T_{0,k}+T'_{{\rm gp},k}{}^j\) |
| Full level \(k\) | \(T_{v,{\rm gp},k}^j\), \(c_p(q_{{\rm gp},k}^j)\) | virtual temperature; \(c_p(q)=c_{pd}(1+\alpha_v q)\) |
| Full level \(k\) | \((\omega/p)_{{\rm gp},k}^j\) | \(P_{{\rm gp},k}^j-\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}C_{k\ell}A_{{\rm gp},\ell}^j\), the discrete form of \(P-\sigma^{-1}\int_0^\sigma A(\sigma')\,d\sigma'\); \(C\) is the vertical integration matrix of [2.6](<2.6-semi-implicit-gravity-wave-solver.md>) |
| Full level \(k\) | \(\delta\Phi_{{\rm gp},k}^j\) | \([G(\mathbf T_v^j-\mathbf T^j)]_{{\rm gp},k}\), the geopotential correction caused by the moist virtual temperature |
| Full level \(k\) | \(B_{\rm gp}^{\rm exp,j}\) | \(K_{\rm gp}^j+\delta\Phi_{{\rm gp},k}^j\), the nonlinear part of the Bernoulli scalar \(B=K+\Phi+T_0\pi\); its spectral coefficients \(\widehat B^{\rm exp,j}\) enter Step 3 as \(+\lambda_n\widehat B^{\rm exp,j}\) |
| Interface \(k+\tfrac12\) | \(\dot\sigma_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}^j\) | \(\sigma_{k+1/2}\mathcal A_{\rm gp}^j-\sum_{\ell=1}^{k}\Delta\sigma_\ell A_{{\rm gp},\ell}^j\) |
| Interface \(k+\tfrac12\) | \(J^T_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}{}^j\) | \(\dot\sigma_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}^j(T'_{{\rm gp},k+1}{}^j-T'_{{\rm gp},k}{}^j)\) |
| Interface \(k+\tfrac12\) | \(J^U_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}{}^j,J^V_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}{}^j\) | \(\dot\sigma_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}^j(U_{{\rm gp},k+1}^j-U_{{\rm gp},k}^j)\) and the analogous expression for \(V\) |
| Interface \(k+\tfrac12\) | \(H^\chi_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}{}^j\) | \(\dot\sigma_{{\rm gp},k+1/2}^j(\chi_{{\rm gp},k}^j+\chi_{{\rm gp},k+1}^j)\) |
| Full level \(k\) | \(F_{U,{\rm gp},k}^j,F_{V,{\rm gp},k}^j\) | momentum combinations from (2.1.9) and (2.1.10), with vertical advection gathered back to full levels through \(J^U,J^V\) |
| Full level \(k\) | \(\mathcal S_{T,{\rm gp},k}^{{\rm exp},j}\) | the explicit part of \(DT'-\dot\sigma\,\partial_\sigma T+\kappa T\omega/p\). The \(D\)-linear reference-profile remainder is added in Step 4 |
| Full level \(k\) | \(\mathcal S_{\chi,{\rm gp},k}^j\) | \(-\partial_\sigma(\dot\sigma\chi)\) |
| Full level \(k\) | horizontal products | \(UT',VT',U\chi,V\chi\), used in Step 3 to compute the horizontal flux divergence |

These interface quantities play different roles. Temperature and momentum are written in advective form, so \(J^T\), \(J^U\), and \(J^V\) are just \(\dot\sigma\) times the jump between two adjacent layers; they are not fluxes themselves. The vertical transport of a layer is obtained by adding the \(J\) values at its two interfaces and dividing by \(2\Delta\sigma_k\) (see [2.4 σ vertical discretization](<2.4-sigma-vertical-discretization.md>)).

Humidity is written in flux form: the interface value is the average of the two adjacent layers, so \(H^\chi/2=\dot\sigma_{k+1/2}(\chi_k+\chi_{k+1})/2\) is a conservative interface flux. The code stores the unhalved sum \(H^\chi\), and the factor \(1/2\) is applied when the layer term is assembled.

Both the top and bottom boundaries take \(\dot\sigma=0\).

**What the Step 2 temperature source does and does not contain.** The table entry above is the full physical composition of \(DT'-\dot\sigma\,\partial_\sigma T+\kappa T\omega/p\), but the array that Step 2 actually fills is only the explicit subset of it. Step 3 analyzes this explicit subset, together with the horizontal flux divergence, into \(\widehat R_{T'}^{\rm exp,j}\). The subset consists of:

- \(DT'\), evaluated at the current level \(j\) and not deferred;
- the vertical transport of \(T'\), using the full current-level \(\dot\sigma\), including the part driven by \(D^j\);
- the vertical transport of the reference profile \(T_0\), keeping only the part driven by the surface-pressure advection \(P=\mathbf v_h\!\cdot\!\nabla_h\pi\), through the `column_pressure_advection` and `cumulative_pressure_advection` terms; the divergence-driven part of that transport is in the semi implicit calculation;
- the non-reference compressional contribution \(\kappa\theta_c\mathcal W\), evaluated with the full \(\mathcal W=P-C(P+\mathbf D)\), where \(\theta_c=T_v/(1+\alpha_vq)-T_0\) and \(\theta_c=T'\) in the dry limit;
- the reference-state compressional contribution \(\kappa T_0\mathcal W_{\rm exp}\), keeping \(\mathcal W_{\rm exp}=P-C(P)\) here and leaving the divergence-driven piece \(-\kappa T_0C\mathbf D\) to the semi-implicit calculation.

Thus the grid-point stage computes

$$
\kappa\theta_c\bigl[P-C(P+\mathbf D)\bigr]
+\kappa T_0\bigl[P-C(P)\bigr],
$$

and the semi-implicit stage supplies the remaining \(-\kappa T_0C\mathbf D\). In the dry limit their sum is the original full-temperature term

$$
\kappa(T_0+T')\bigl[P-C(P+\mathbf D)\bigr]
=\kappa T\frac{\omega}{p}.
$$

**Step 3: Form the explicit spectral tendencies**

For each \((n,m,k)\),

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\widehat R_\zeta^{\rm j})_{n,k}^m
&=\widehat{\operatorname{curl}_h(F_{U,\rm gp}^j,F_{V,\rm gp}^j)}{}_{n,k}^m,\\
(\widehat R_D^{\rm exp,j})_{n,k}^m
&=\widehat{\operatorname{div}_h(F_{U,\rm gp}^j,F_{V,\rm gp}^j)}{}_{n,k}^m
  +\lambda_n(\widehat B^{\rm exp,j})_{n,k}^m,\\
(\widehat R_{T'}^{\rm exp,j})_{n,k}^m
    &=\widehat{\mathcal S_{T,\rm gp}^{{\rm exp},j}}{}_{n,k}^m
  -\widehat{\operatorname{div}_h(U_{\rm gp}^jT'_{\rm gp}{}^j,
                                  V_{\rm gp}^jT'_{\rm gp}{}^j)}{}_{n,k}^m,\\
(\widehat R_\chi^{\rm j})_{n,k}^m
&=\widehat{\mathcal S_{\chi,\rm gp}^j}{}_{n,k}^m
  -\widehat{\operatorname{div}_h(U_{\rm gp}^j\chi_{\rm gp}^j,
                                  V_{\rm gp}^j\chi_{\rm gp}^j)}{}_{n,k}^m.
\end{aligned}
$$

The products and fluxes on the right-hand side are first formed in grid space; the outer hats indicate that the result is analyzed into spectral coefficients only in Step 3. The superscript \({\rm exp}\) is short for explicit, not for the exponential function \(\exp(\cdot)\). \(\widehat R_D^{\rm exp,j}\) and \(\widehat R_{T'}^{\rm exp,j}\) do not yet include the fast linear coupling of Step 4; vorticity and humidity do not take part in that coupling, so their explicit results already give the complete adiabatic right-hand sides.

The complete Bernoulli scalar in (2.1.11) is \(B=K+\Phi+T_0\pi\). Here \(B_{\rm gp}^{\rm exp,j}\) refers only to the nonlinear part formed at the grid-point stage, and \(+\lambda_n(\widehat B^{\rm exp,j})_{n,k}^{m}\) is the spectral representation of \(-\nabla_h^2B^{\rm exp}\) (because \(-\nabla_h^2\) acts as multiplication by \(\lambda_n\) on a spherical harmonic). Do not confuse the scalar \(B\) with the vertical matrix \(B_n\) cached in Step 4.

**Step 4: Semi-implicit solve for the fast gravity waves**

![Derivation of the semi-implicit gravity-wave solver](<../assets/images/semi_implicit.png>)

*Figure 2.2.1 | Workflow of the semi-implicit solver.*

The fast linear coupling among \(\widehat D\), \(\widehat{T'}\), and \(\widehat\pi\) cannot be treated fully explicitly as in Step 3. Its time-centered implicit treatment is the classical semi-implicit spectral-model method (Kwizak & Robert, 1971; Hoskins & Simmons, 1975). At initialization, the program builds an \(L\times L\) vertical coupling matrix \(M_n\) for each \(n=1,\ldots,N\) and caches its inverse \(B_n\):

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_n
&=
\frac{1}{\lambda_n}I
+\Delta\tau^2
\left(
\mathbf T_0\,\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma^{\mathsf T}
+G\mathcal T_D
\right),\\
B_n&=M_n^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here the symbols have the following roles:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| \(L\) | number of model vertical levels; hence \(M_n,B_n,I,G,\mathcal T_D\) are all \(L\times L\) matrices |
| \(M_n\) | coefficient matrix of the vertical linear system for total wavenumber \(n\) |
| \(B_n=M_n^{-1}\) | solve matrix inverted and cached at initialization |
| \(I\) | identity matrix |
| \(\Delta\tau\) | dimensionless time step |
| \(\mathbf T_0\) | \(L\)-dimensional column vector of the reference temperatures of the levels |
| \(\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma\) | \(L\)-dimensional column vector of level thicknesses \(\Delta\sigma_k\); the superscript \({\mathsf T}\) denotes the transpose to a row vector |
| \(G\) | hydrostatic integration matrix, converting a temperature perturbation into a geopotential perturbation |
| \(\mathcal T_D\) | vertical coupling matrix for the linear divergence contribution to the temperature-perturbation tendency, with \(\mathbf R_{T'}^{\rm lin}=-\mathcal T_D\mathbf D\) |
| \(G\mathcal T_D\) | gravity-wave feedback along "divergence \(\rightarrow\) temperature \(\rightarrow\) geopotential \(\rightarrow\) divergence" |

At time level \(j\), for each \((n,m)\) the program performs three operations.

- Assemble the right-hand side \(\widehat{\mathbf b}_n^{m,j}\).

- Solve for the semi-implicit divergence. One matrix–vector product

$$
\overline{\widehat{\mathbf D}}_{\rm SI,n}^{m,j}
=B_n\widehat{\mathbf b}_n^{m,j},
$$

gives the semi-implicit time-centered spectral divergence on all \(L\) levels of that mode. The overbar here denotes the time center between \(\widetilde{\widehat{\mathbf D}}{}^{j-1}\) and the candidate new level \(\widehat{\mathbf D}_*^{j+1}\).

- Write back the coupled tendencies. The same divergence solution is used to complete the linear tendencies of column mass and temperature:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\widehat{\mathcal A}^{j})_n^m
&=(\widehat{\mathcal A}^{\rm exp,j})_n^m
+\Delta\boldsymbol\sigma^{\mathsf T}
 \overline{\widehat{\mathbf D}}_{\rm SI,n}^{m,j},\\
(\widehat{\mathbf R}_{T'}^{j})_n^m
&=(\widehat{\mathbf R}_{T'}^{\rm exp,j})_n^m
-\mathcal T_D\overline{\widehat{\mathbf D}}_{\rm SI,n}^{m,j}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This step therefore does not "guess" \(D,T',\pi\) separately. It first obtains, through \(B_n\), a full column of spectral divergence under the joint constraint of the three variables, and then consistently completes \(\widehat{\mathcal A}^{j}\) and \(\widehat{\mathbf R}_{T'}^{j}\). The matrix depends only on \(n\), so all zonal wavenumbers \(m\) with the same \(n\) share one \(B_n\).

\(n=0\) has no invertible horizontal Laplacian and does not enter this solver; its constant \(\pi\) mode is fixed separately as the global mass reference.

**Step 5: Form the adiabatic candidate state**

For \(X\in\{\zeta,T',\chi\}\), the adiabatic leapfrog candidate spectral coefficients are

$$
\widehat X_*^{j+1}
=\widetilde{\widehat X}^{\,j-1}
+2\Delta\tau\widehat R_X^j.
$$

Divergence is treated slightly differently: Step 4 returns a semi-implicit time-centered spectral value:

$$
\overline{\widehat D}_{\rm SI}^{j}
\equiv
\frac{\widetilde{\widehat D}^{\,j-1}+\widehat D_*^{j+1}}{2},
\qquad
\widehat D_*^{j+1}
=2\overline{\widehat D}_{\rm SI}^{j}
-\widetilde{\widehat D}^{\,j-1}.
$$

The log surface pressure is updated with the column-integral spectral coefficients completed in Step 4:

$$
\widehat\pi_*^{j+1}
=\widetilde{\widehat\pi}^{\,j-1}
-2\Delta\tau\widehat{\mathcal A}^{j}.
$$

At this point, for the five prognostic spectral variables, the code saves only the part of the Robert–Asselin filter that does not depend on the final new level:

$$
\widetilde{\widehat X}_{\rm partial}^{j}
=\nu\widetilde{\widehat X}^{\,j-1}
+(1-2\nu)\widehat X^j,
\qquad
X\in\{\zeta,D,T',\pi,\chi\}.
$$

Physics and dissipation will continue to modify the candidate new level, so the term \(+\nu\widehat X^{j+1}\) required by the filter is deferred to Step 10.

**Step 6: Recover the state required by the physical processes**

The adiabatic candidate spectral state from Step 5 is synthesized with spherical harmonics, and at each grid point the prognostic-variable definitions give

$$
\left(\frac{p_s}{p_0}\right)_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}
=e^{\pi_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}}
$$

$$
T_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}=T_0+T'_{*,\rm gp}{}^{j+1}
$$

$$
u_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}=\frac{U_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}}{\cos\varphi}
$$

$$
v_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}=\frac{V_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}}{\cos\varphi}
$$

$$
q_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}
=\chi_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}e^{-\pi_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}}
$$

Multiplying by the fixed scales \(p_0\), \(u_*=a\Omega\), and \(T_*=a^2\Omega^2/R_d\) then gives the \(p_s\,[\mathrm{Pa}]\), \(T\,[\mathrm K]\), \((u,v)\,[\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}]\), and \(q\,[\mathrm{kg\,kg^{-1}}]\) that the parameterizations actually receive. The time label here is still the candidate new level \(*^{j+1}\), not the current level \(j\) used in Steps 1–3 to compute the adiabatic tendencies.

**Step 7: Compute the physical sources and sinks**

Parameterizations such as radiation, boundary-layer, convection, and moist processes compute

$$
\frac{du}{dt},\qquad
\frac{dv}{dt},\qquad
\frac{dT}{dt},\qquad
\frac{dq}{dt}
$$

from the state of Step 6. These dimensional tendencies are then divided by the corresponding variable scale, which turns them into dimensionless grid-point tendencies. Below, the superscript \({\rm phys},*\) means "physical tendency evaluated on the candidate state \(X_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}\)", so that it is not mistaken for the current level \(j\):

$$
r_{u,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*},\qquad
r_{v,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*},\qquad
r_{T',\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*},\qquad
r_{\chi,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*}.
$$

For humidity, the mass weight must be included as well:

$$
r_{\chi,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*}
=\left.\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial\tau}\right|_{\rm phys}
=\left(\frac{p_s}{p_0}\right)_{*,\rm gp}^{j+1}\frac{1}{\Omega}
  \left.\frac{\partial q}{\partial t}\right|_{\rm phys}.
$$

Because \(T_0\) is fixed, the physics \(dT/dt\) is also \(dT'/dt\). In a dynamics-only run, all four physical tendencies remain zero.

**Step 8: Transform the physical tendencies into spectral space**

The grid-point wind tendencies are treated as a tangential vector field on the sphere, \(\mathbf r_{v,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*}=(r_{u,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*},r_{v,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*})\); a single vector spherical harmonic analysis gives both the divergence and vorticity tendencies. Temperature and mass-weighted humidity are analyzed as scalars:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\widehat R_D^{{\rm phys},*})_{n,k}^m
&=\widehat{\nabla_h\!\cdot\!\mathbf r_{v,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*}}{}_{n,k}^m,
&
(\widehat R_\zeta^{{\rm phys},*})_{n,k}^m
&=\widehat{\operatorname{curl}_h\mathbf r_{v,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*}}{}_{n,k}^m,\\
(\widehat R_{T'}^{{\rm phys},*})_{n,k}^m
&=\widehat{r_{T',\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*}}{}_{n,k}^m,
&
(\widehat R_\chi^{{\rm phys},*})_{n,k}^m
&=\widehat{r_{\chi,\rm gp}^{{\rm phys},*}}{}_{n,k}^m.
\end{aligned}
$$


**Step 9: Friction, Newtonian cooling, and high-order spectral dissipation**

For \(X\in\{\zeta,D,T',\chi\}\), the state variables and tendencies in the equation below are spectral coefficients:

$$
\widehat X_{\rm phys}^{j+1}
=\widehat X_*^{j+1}
+2\Delta\tau\widehat R_X^{{\rm phys},*}.
$$

The superscript \(*\) emphasizes that this tendency was evaluated on the candidate grid state. Physical parameterizations do not change \(\widehat\pi\) directly. 

The dynamical core then applies its internal damping: Rayleigh friction, Newtonian cooling, and high-order spectral dissipation. Such damping terms are treated implicitly (Durran, 2010): a mode damped at a rate \(a_{X,n,k}\ge0\) is multiplied by the exact factor

$$
(\widehat X^{j+1})_{n,k}^{m}
=\frac{(\widehat X_{\rm phys}^{j+1})_{n,k}^{m}}
       {1+2\Delta\tau a_{X,n,k}},
$$

so damping can only reduce the amplitude and can never destabilize the time step, regardless of \(2\Delta\tau a_{X,n,k}\). Which damping mechanisms act on which variable is summarized below; the exact expressions are given in [2.9 Spectral Dissipation](<2.9-spectral-dissipation.md>).

| Variable | Damping mechanisms|
|---|---|
| \(\chi\), mass-weighted humidity | spectral dissipation \(d_{\chi,n,k}\) | 
| \(D\), divergence | Rayleigh friction \(r_{F,k}\) + spectral dissipation \(d_{D,n,k}\) |
| \(\zeta\), vorticity | Rayleigh friction \(r_{F,k}\) + spectral dissipation \(d_{\zeta,n,k}\) | 
| \(T'\), temperature perturbation | Newtonian relaxation \(r_{T,k}\) + spectral dissipation \(d_{T,n,k}\) | 
| \(\pi\), log surface pressure | none | 

Step 9 also contains a dissipation-heating path (Boville & Bretherton, 2003): it compares the wind field before and after damping and returns the removed kinetic energy as heat. It is not yet activated inside the current time step; see [2.9](<2.9-spectral-dissipation.md>).

**Step 10: Complete the filter and rotate the time levels**

After the physical processes and dissipation, \(\widehat X^{j+1}\) is the complete new time level. Now the last term of the Robert–Asselin filter (Robert, 1966; Asselin, 1972) is added for \(X\in\{\zeta,D,T',\pi,\chi\}\):

$$
\widetilde{\widehat X}^{\,j}
=\widetilde{\widehat X}_{\rm partial}^{j}
+\nu\widehat X^{j+1}
=\nu\widetilde{\widehat X}^{\,j-1}
+(1-2\nu)\widehat X^j
+\nu\widehat X^{j+1}.
$$

If startup short steps are enabled, the filter is not completed in the standard way until they end (in the current C runtime the number of short steps is zero, so normal leapfrog integration starts immediately). The code also fixes the \((m=0,n=0)\) mode of \(\widehat\pi\) as the global mass reference, and checks every spectral coefficient of \(\widehat\zeta,\widehat D,\widehat{T'},\widehat\pi,\widehat\chi\) for finiteness. After these checks pass, the program rotates the two spectral time levels forward: it keeps the complete new state \(\widehat X^{j+1}\), which computes the tendencies at time \(j+1\) in the next step, and it keeps the filtered \(\widetilde{\widehat X}^{\,j}\), which serves as the old-level starting point of the next leapfrog update. The explicit part of the next step therefore has the form

$$
\widehat X^{j+2}
=\widetilde{\widehat X}^{\,j}
+2\Delta\tau\widehat R_X^{j+1}.
$$

# 2.8 Spectral Dissipation

This note continues the variable and time-level notation of the dynamical-core entry note
([2.1 Governing Equations](<2.1-governing-equations.md>)) and checks the actual update order of
`spectrald_add_physics`, `spectrald_dissipation`, and `spectrald_finalize`. It also distinguishes
two things that are easy to confuse: auxiliary operators that have been implemented in the source,
and operators that the current runtime actually calls.

## 1. Where damping enters a time step

Write

$$
\delta\tau=2\Delta\tau,
$$

where \(\Delta\tau\) is the dimensionless leapfrog time step
(see [2.6 Leapfrog Time Stepping](<2.6-leapfrog-time-stepping.md>)).
For \(X\in\{\eta,D,T',\chi\}\), the program first adds the physical tendency to the adiabatic candidate new level:

$$
\widehat X_{\rm in}
=\widehat X_*^{j+1}
+\delta\tau\,\widehat R_X^{{\rm phys},*}.
\tag{2.8.1}
$$

Here \(\eta=\zeta+f\) is the absolute vorticity. The current implementation stores \(\eta\) in the
spectral array, not the relative vorticity \(\zeta\) alone; the program subtracts the planetary
vorticity only when it inverts the wind field, from the real coefficient of the \((m=0,n=1)\) mode.
Below, \(\widehat X_{\rm in}\) always denotes a spectral coefficient after the physical processes
have been added but before damping.

`spectrald_dissipation` computes the damping tendencies from \(\widehat X_{\rm in}\), and
`spectrald_finalize` then performs

$$
\widehat X_{\rm out}
=\widehat X_{\rm in}
+\delta\tau\,\widehat R_X^{\rm damp}.
\tag{2.8.2}
$$

Damping terms are applied implicitly, i.e. with backward Euler (Durran, 2010): a single mechanism
with rate \(a\ge0\) gives

$$
\widehat R_X^{\rm damp}
=-\frac{a\,\widehat X_{\rm in}}{1+\delta\tau a},
\qquad
\widehat X_{\rm out}
=\frac{\widehat X_{\rm in}}{1+\delta\tau a}.
$$

The implicit factor lies between zero and one for any step size, so it can only reduce the
amplitude and does not suffer the stability restriction of explicit damping. The subsections below
apply this prototype variable by variable: humidity uses it directly, divergence and vorticity add
two such terms with different rates, and temperature places both rates in one denominator.

The log surface pressure \(\pi\) does not take part in this damping step. The root process also
fixes its global-mean mode to zero, so that the global mass reference does not drift.

## 2. How the spectral dissipation rates are constructed at initialization

### 2.1 High-wavenumber selection function

For total wavenumber \(n\) and level \(k\), `initpm_spectral` constructs

$$
L_{n,k}
=\begin{cases}
0, & n<n_h,\\[4pt]
\left(\dfrac{n-n_h}{N-n_h}\right)^{p_k}, & n\ge n_h,
\end{cases}
\tag{2.8.3}
$$

where \(N\) is the truncation total wavenumber, \(n_h\) corresponds to `nhdiff`, and \(p_k\)
corresponds to `ndel[k]` at level \(k\). For the current default of the positive integer
\(p_k=2\), \(L_{n_h,k}=0\) and \(L_{N,k}=1\); increasing \(p_k\) concentrates the dissipation more
strongly in the tail of the truncation. This scale-selective dissipation form comes from Laursen
and Eliasen (1989) and was adopted by ECHAM (Roeckner et al., 1992; for a review see Jablonowski
& Williamson, 2011).


### 2.2 Time scales and dimensionless rates

All damping time scales (`tdissd/tdissz/tdisst/tdissq`, `restim`, `tfrc`) are in **days**; the code
performs no unit check or conversion, so the person configuring the model is responsible for the
units. `initpm_spectral` converts a positive time scale \(\tau_{X,k}\) (in days) into the
dimensionless rate

$$
h_{X,k}
=\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{X,k}},
\qquad
\tau_{X,k}>0,
\tag{2.8.4}
$$

whereas a non-positive value gives a zero damping rate. The factor \(2\pi\) appears because the
model time is dimensionless, \(\tau=\Omega t\) (see
[2.1 Governing Equations](<2.1-governing-equations.md>)): one day corresponds to \(2\pi\) model
time units, so a process with time scale \(\tau_{X,k}\) days has the rate
\(1/(2\pi\tau_{X,k})\). The spectral dissipation rate is finally

$$
d_{X,n,k}=h_{X,k}L_{n,k},
\qquad X\in\{D,\zeta,T',\chi\}.
\tag{2.8.5}
$$

Because \(L_{N,k}=1\), the input `tdiss*` is the dissipation time scale at the spectral truncation
scale. The level-wise rates of Rayleigh friction and Newtonian relaxation are obtained with the
same conversion (for the standard setup of Rayleigh friction and Newtonian cooling see Held &
Suarez, 1994):

$$
r_{F,k}=\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{F,k}},
\qquad
r_{T,k}=\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{R,k}}.
\tag{2.8.6}
$$

The current built-in defaults reflect these implementation details:

| Quantity | Current default | Effect |
|---|---:|---|
| `nhdiff` | 15 | no spectral dissipation for \(n<15\) |
| `ndel[k]` | 2 | quadratic tail-selection function at every level |
| `tdissd[k]` | 0.20 d | divergence dissipation at the truncation scale |
| `tdissz[k]` | 1.10 d | vorticity dissipation at the truncation scale |
| `tdisst[k]` | 5.60 d | temperature dissipation at the truncation scale |
| `tdissq[k]` | 0.10 d | mass-weighted-humidity dissipation at the truncation scale |
| `restim[k]` | 0 | Newtonian relaxation switched off |
| `nsponge`, `dampsp` | 0, 0 | top-level sponge switched off |

**Top-level Rayleigh friction.** 
It sets the time scale from the height of the damped layer: levels whose \(\sigma\) interface is at
most 0.095 receive 20 days, and levels whose interface is at most 0.17 receive 100 days; all deeper
levels keep zero. For the 10-level grid this reproduces the classic preset (20 d and 100 d on the
top two levels) exactly, and on a finer grid the damped layer extends downward, so that the same
mass fraction near the model top is covered. This preset acts on divergence and vorticity as
spectral Rayleigh friction at the model top; it is not grid-point drag near the surface. The
current spectral Rayleigh friction should therefore not be described loosely as "near-surface
damping".

## 3. Exact update of each prognostic variable

### 3.1 Temperature: relaxation and spectral dissipation share one denominator

Let \(\widehat T'_R\) be the spectral coefficients of the target restoration temperature
perturbation, let \(d_{T,n,k}=h_{T,k}L_{n,k}\) be the scale-selective dissipation rate of total
wavenumber \(n\) at level \(k\), and define the combined rate

$$
a_{T,n,k}=r_{T,k}+d_{T,n,k},
$$

in which \(r_{T,k}\) relaxes level \(k\) toward the restoration profile and \(d_{T,n,k}\) grows
from zero at low wavenumbers to \(h_{T,k}\) at the truncation scale (equation 2.8.3). The program
computes

$$
\widehat R_{T'}^{\rm damp}
=\frac{-a_{T,n,k}\widehat T'_{\rm in}
       +r_{T,k}\widehat T'_R}
       {1+\delta\tau a_{T,n,k}},
\tag{2.8.7}
$$

so

$$
\widehat T'_{\rm out}
=\frac{\widehat T'_{\rm in}
       +\delta\tau r_{T,k}\widehat T'_R}
       {1+\delta\tau a_{T,n,k}}.
\tag{2.8.8}
$$

If \(d_{T,n,k}=0\), this is a backward Euler update toward \(\widehat T'_R\) (Durran, 2010); if
\(r_{T,k}=0\), it reduces to pure spectral dissipation. Temperature is the only variable that
combines the two rates in a single denominator.

At runtime the restoration temperature is read from the restart array `sr`, whose rank-local
spectral block `srp` is passed to `spectrald_dissipation` as `restoration_temperature`. 
On a cold start `sr` therefore keeps its initial zero
value. With the default `restim=0` the relaxation rate is zero anyway, so the target field cannot
affect the time integration.

### 3.2 Mass-weighted humidity: pure spectral dissipation

The humidity spectral array stores \(\chi=(p_s/p_0)q\), so \(\chi\) is what gets damped, not the
specific humidity \(q\) itself. With \(d_{\chi,n,k}=h_{\chi,k}L_{n,k}\),

$$
\widehat R_\chi^{\rm H}
=-\frac{d_{\chi,n,k}\widehat\chi_{\rm in}}
        {1+\delta\tau d_{\chi,n,k}},
\qquad
\widehat\chi_{\rm out}
=\frac{\widehat\chi_{\rm in}}
       {1+\delta\tau d_{\chi,n,k}}.
\tag{2.8.9}
$$

This is exactly the prototype implicit update with the single rate \(a=d_{\chi,n,k}\), so humidity
is the one variable for which a single damping factor is exact.

### 3.3 Divergence and vorticity: friction and spectral dissipation are made implicit separately

For divergence, the code computes

$$
\widehat R_D^{\rm F}
=-\frac{r_{F,k}\widehat D_{\rm in}}
        {1+\delta\tau r_{F,k}},
\qquad
\widehat R_D^{\rm H}
=-\frac{d_{D,n,k}\widehat D_{\rm in}}
        {1+\delta\tau d_{D,n,k}},
\tag{2.8.10}
$$

and then sets \(\widehat R_D^{\rm damp}=\widehat R_D^{\rm F}+\widehat R_D^{\rm H}\). The exact
amplification factor of one update is therefore

$$
\widehat D_{\rm out}
=\left[
\frac{1}{1+\delta\tau r_{F,k}}
+\frac{1}{1+\delta\tau d_{D,n,k}}
-1
\right]\widehat D_{\rm in}.
\tag{2.8.11}
$$

This is **not** equal to
\(\widehat D_{\rm in}/[1+\delta\tau(r_{F,k}+d_{D,n,k})]\). Only when one of the two rates is zero
does equation (2.8.11) reduce to the standard backward Euler update for a single damping term.

The vorticity update uses the same two denominators, but its damping acts on the relative
vorticity. Let \(\widehat f\) denote the planetary-vorticity spectral coefficients, which are
nonzero only in the real part of \((m=0,n=1)\); then
\(\widehat\zeta_{\rm in}=\widehat\eta_{\rm in}-\widehat f\), and the special-mode correction in the
source gives

$$
\widehat R_\eta^{\rm F}
=-\frac{r_{F,k}\widehat\zeta_{\rm in}}
        {1+\delta\tau r_{F,k}},
\qquad
\widehat R_\eta^{\rm H}
=-\frac{d_{\zeta,n,k}\widehat\zeta_{\rm in}}
        {1+\delta\tau d_{\zeta,n,k}}.
\tag{2.8.12}
$$

In other words, the program does not treat the fixed vorticity of planetary rotation as
dissipatable circulation. The updated \(\widehat\zeta\) has the same amplification factor as
equation (2.8.11), with \(d_D\) replaced by \(d_\zeta\).

## 4. Top-level sponge

If `nsponge>0`, the program adds the following to the temperature damping tendency (the classic
treatment of a top sponge layer is given by Klemp & Lilly, 1978; for a review see Jablonowski & Williamson, 2011):

$$
\widehat R_{T',1}^{\rm sponge}
=s\left(\widehat T'_{\rm in,2}
-\widehat T'_{\rm in,1}\right),
\qquad s=\texttt{dampsp}.
\tag{2.8.13}
$$

It modifies the top level only, it uses the temperature **perturbation** coefficients of the top
and second levels before damping, and it is added explicitly through equation (2.8.2). The
complete update of the top level is therefore

$$
\widehat T'_{\rm out,1}
=\frac{\widehat T'_{\rm in,1}
       +\delta\tau r_{T,1}\widehat T'_{R,1}}
       {1+\delta\tau(r_{T,1}+d_{T,n,1})}
+\delta\tau s
 \left(\widehat T'_{\rm in,2}-\widehat T'_{\rm in,1}\right).
\tag{2.8.14}
$$

## 5. Dissipation heating

If a damping operator changes the kinetic energy per unit mass of the grid-point horizontal wind
by

$$
\Delta K_k
=\frac12\left[
(u_{k,\rm after}^2+v_{k,\rm after}^2)
-(u_{k,\rm before}^2+v_{k,\rm before}^2)
\right],
\tag{2.8.15}
$$

then the ideal heating tendency is

$$
\left.\frac{\partial T'_k}{\partial t}\right|_{\rm diss}
=-\frac{\Delta K_k}
        {\delta t\,c_{pd}(1+\alpha_v q_k)},
\tag{2.8.16}
$$

where \(\delta t\) is the dimensional time interval spanned by this wind update and
\(c_p(q)=c_{pd}(1+\alpha_v q)\). When \(\Delta K_k<0\), equation (2.8.16) is positive, meaning that
the dissipated kinetic energy is converted into internal energy (for a discussion of dissipation
heating see Boville & Bretherton, 2003). Because the reference temperature profile does not change
with time, adding the same tendency to the total temperature and to the temperature perturbation
is equivalent.

Equation (2.8.16) is a grid-point diagnosis: the wind fields before and after the damping
operators are compared at each \((\lambda,\varphi)\) of level \(k\), and the result is the
grid-point field \(r^{\rm diss}_{T',{\rm gp},k}\), not a spectral tendency. To enter the spectral
temperature equation it must first be expanded in spherical harmonics,
\(\widehat R_{T'}^{\rm diss}=\widehat{r^{\rm diss}_{T',{\rm gp}}}\), which gives one coefficient
per mode \((n,m,k)\).

Earlier revisions defined auxiliary operators `mkdheat_friction` and `mkdheat_diffusion_*` that
diagnosed, according to equations (2.8.15)–(2.8.16), the wind changes caused by spectral friction
and spectral dissipation; the diffusion variant carried the layer mass \(p_s\Delta\sigma_k/g\)
explicitly in its intermediate calculation and divided it out again when converting to a
temperature tendency per unit mass. These operators are not part of the current kernel set and
have no call site in the time step. Equation (2.8.16) therefore describes the design meaning of
the intended auxiliary operator, not an energy feedback that is active in the current
`runtime_spectral_diabatic` step. The dissipation heating that is active in the model belongs to the
physics (surface friction and vertical diffusion, controlled by `ndheat`); see
[3.3 Horizontal and Vertical Diffusion](<../3-parameterizations/3.3-horizontal-and-vertical-diffusion.md>).

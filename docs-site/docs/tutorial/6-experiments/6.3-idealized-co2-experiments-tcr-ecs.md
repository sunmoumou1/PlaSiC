# 6.3 1pctCO₂ and abrupt-4×CO₂ experiments { #co2-sensitivity-experiments }

The CMIP6 DECK includes two idealized experiments that isolate the climate response to prescribed CO₂: `1pctCO2` and `abrupt-4xCO2`. PlaSiC runs both with a coupled ocean, branching from the same initial state as a fixed-CO₂ control run that supplies the reference climate.

## 1. Experimental design

| Component | PlaSiC setting |
|---|---|
| Atmosphere | T21; 64 × 32 Gaussian grid; 10 σ levels |
| Ocean | 50 m mixed layer coupled to a 16-level three-dimensional ocean |
| Integration | 45 min per step; 32 steps per day; Gregorian calendar |
| Initial state | Branch A from the 500-year, fixed-CO₂ ocean spin-up |
| Control | Pre-industrial CO₂ (284.316853 ppm) held fixed for 150 years, 1850–1999, from the same initial state |
| Reference | Linear trend of the control's annual means over 1850–1999, evaluated at the same year (Section 2) |
| Forcing | Prescribed, spatially uniform CO₂ only; no other time-varying forcing |
| `1pctCO2` | CO₂ rises by 1% yr⁻¹, compounded monthly, for 140 years; it reaches approximately 4× the initial concentration |
| `abrupt-4xCO2` | CO₂ is stepped immediately to 4× (about 1,137 ppm) and held there for 150 years |
| Output | One model member; monthly fields |

For the gradual experiment, the prescribed concentration is approximately

$$
C(t) = C_0(1.01)^t,
$$

where $t$ is elapsed time in years. It doubles after about 70 years. The abrupt experiment applies the same eventual 4× concentration at the start, so the ocean initially takes up heat much faster.

## 2. How are TCR and ECS calculated?

First calculate annual global means and anomalies relative to the control. The fixed-CO₂ control runs for the same 150 years. A straight line is fitted to each of its annual-mean series over 1850–1999 (for maps and profiles, at every grid cell and level), and each experiment year is compared with that line at the same calendar year. This removes the control's drift as well as its mean state, as for the CMIP6 reference values in Section 4. Let $\Delta T$ be the change in global-mean near-surface air temperature (`tas`) and let $\Delta N$ be the change in net downward top-of-atmosphere (TOA) radiation, $\Delta N = \Delta rsdt - \Delta rsut - \Delta rlut$.

The control is not in exact equilibrium. Its initial state comes from the 500-year spin-up, which was made before the November 2026 fixes described in Section 3, and the corrected model drifts away from it: the global-mean `tas` of the control falls from 287.11 K in 1850–1859 to 286.89 K in 1990–1999, a linear trend of −0.25 K per century, and varies by 0.08 K (one standard deviation) about that trend. Its diagnosed net downward TOA flux stays near +3.6 W m⁻² even though it cools slightly, so the model's global energy budget does not close exactly; taking $\Delta N$ relative to the control removes this offset. With the fixed 1850–1859 control mean as the reference instead of the trend, TCR would be 1.99 K and the effective ECS 5.21 K ($R^2=0.869$); `summary.json` reports both.

### Transient Climate Response (TCR)

TCR measures warming while CO₂ is still rising. In the standard `1pctCO2` protocol, it is the 20-year mean temperature anomaly over years 61–80, centred near the time CO₂ doubles:

$$
\mathrm{TCR} = \frac{1}{20}\sum_{y=61}^{80} \Delta T_y.
$$

Because the ocean has not equilibrated, TCR reflects both climate feedbacks and continuing ocean heat uptake. The CMIP6 reference table below uses this 61–80-year definition.

The local annual arrays label 1850 as relative year 0, so the analysis script selects relative years 60–79 (calendar years 1910–1929). These are the protocol's years 61–80.

### Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS)

ECS is the equilibrium global warming after a doubling of CO₂. The `abrupt-4xCO2` run is not long enough to reach equilibrium, so an *effective* ECS is estimated with a Gregory regression. The run covers 1850–1999, but the current analysis excludes the first annual mean and regresses the 149 annual means from 1851–1999:

$$
\Delta N = F_{4\times} + \lambda\Delta T,
\qquad
\mathrm{ECS}_{\mathrm{eff}} = -\frac{F_{4\times}}{2\lambda}.
$$

Here $F_{4\times}$ is the fitted intercept (effective forcing for 4×CO₂), and $\lambda$ is the fitted feedback slope, normally negative. Setting $\Delta N=0$ gives the extrapolated equilibrium warming for 4×CO₂, $-F_{4\times}/\lambda$; dividing by two gives the warming for one CO₂ doubling. Because the regression uses a transient response, its effective ECS can depend on the years fitted and on the evolving warming pattern.

The figure labels the halved intercept, $F_{2\times}=F_{4\times}/2$, which is about 4.04 W m⁻² for this 149-point fit.

## 3. PlaSiC results

| Diagnostic | PlaSiC result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| TCR (`1pctCO2`) | 2.04 K | 20-year mean for relative years 60–79 (calendar years 1910–1929; protocol years 61–80) |
| `1pctCO2` warming in year 140 | 4.70 K | Final model year (1989); CO₂ is approximately 4× its initial concentration |
| `abrupt-4xCO2` warming in year 150 | 5.48 K | Final model year (1999); the surface continues to warm during the run |
| Gregory intercept, $F_{4\times}$ | 8.09 W m⁻² | Effective forcing inferred from the 1851–1999 regression |
| Gregory slope, $\lambda$ | −0.806 W m⁻² K⁻¹ | Negative feedback slope, as expected for a stabilizing climate response |
| Effective ECS | 5.01 K | From the 149-point 1851–1999 regression; $R^2=0.905$ |

The Gregory method therefore extrapolates beyond the simulated temperatures rather than treating the final-year warming as ECS. The vertical response also shows tropospheric warming, stratospheric cooling and ocean warming that weakens with depth. At T21 resolution, regional map details are diagnostic only.

These results come from runs made after two November 2026 fixes. The first corrected the sea-ice compactness (see [Figure 4.3.1, step 6](../4-model-and-coupling/4.3-sea-ice-module.md)). Before it, $c_i^*$ was reset to the 0/1 ice mask every step, so new ice could not form and the Arctic ice cover could only shrink: it disappeared, in winter as well as in summer, after about 20 years of `abrupt-4xCO2` and about 70 years of `1pctCO2`. That spurious loss of sea ice amplified the Arctic warming and gave TCR = 3.29 K and an effective ECS of 5.59 K. The second corrected the exchange of the three-dimensional ocean's ghost rows between MPI ranks (see [4.5 §4.5](../4-model-and-coupling/4.5-three-dimensional-ocean.md#45-ghost-rows-at-process-boundaries)), so the results no longer depend on the number of ranks. With both fixes, the Arctic keeps a seasonal ice cover. In the control the annual maximum sea-ice area of the Northern Hemisphere stays near 29 million km² and the annual minimum near 9–10 million km². In `1pctCO2` the maximum falls from about 29 to 20 million km² by the last decade and the minimum from about 9 to below 1 million km² (0.3–1.2 million km² in individual years). In `abrupt-4xCO2` the late-summer Arctic becomes nearly ice-free (about 0.1 million km²), while about 17 million km² of ice still forms each winter.

Fig. 6.3.4. **Arctic amplification.** Annual-mean surface air temperature change relative to the control's linear trend at the same grid cell, in year 70 of `1pctCO2` (close to CO₂ doubling) and year 140 of `abrupt-4xCO2`. ## 5. Reproducing the experiments

From the repository root, run:

```bash
# 150-year fixed-CO2 control, 1pctCO2 and abrupt-4xCO2 from the same initial state;
# PLASIC_MPI=1 runs each case on PLASIC_NPRO MPI ranks (default 2); the results
# do not depend on the number of ranks
PLASIC_MPI=1 bash experiments/co2_4xCO2_1pctCO2/run_experiments.sh
# TCR, Gregory regression and figures (summary.json, annual_global_means.csv)
python experiments/co2_4xCO2_1pctCO2/plots/plot_co2_experiments.py
```
